# Which frontier is worth building?

Decision memo · November 24, 2026

**Choose Trophic Arena for the portfolio. Test it with an organizer who already has a room full of people.** A university quant club, AI hackathon or indie-game meetup can run an artificial-life forecasting competition in one evening. Participants read the opening of an ecosystem, enter probabilities and compete against a bot. The organizer can evaluate the entire product in one meeting. The core experiment is whether a second organizer will pay for roughly the same evening.

**Fastest likely route to cash with a warm buyer:** a fixed-price operational audit. A business that can hand over a messy input file and name the hours lost can buy a useful result before the integration is automated. We found evidence that this work exists, particularly in solar. We have no evidence of a warm buyer here. [Sandia provider survey](https://pvpmc.sandia.gov/pvmac/pv-operations-software-transparency-a-pvmac-industry-snapshot/)

**Fastest paid experiment among the interests named:** a hosted game for an existing group. Goosechase's published US single-event tiers include $99 for 10 people, $299 for 40 and $499 for 100. A single organizer can make the purchasing decision. These are prices for a different group experience. Whether an organizer would pay for a digital ecology tournament, and how much it would cost to acquire that organizer, are open questions. [Goosechase event pricing](https://support.goosechase.com/en/articles/4571290-how-much-does-a-goosechase-experience-cost)

**Strongest evidence for recurring revenue:** specialized defense-adjacent procurement or financial research. GovTribe lists $1,900/year for one seat. BamSEC lists $69/month billed annually. For a second build, I would target the person at a small aerospace supplier who checks solicitation amendments. Reconstruct what changed, cite the previous and revised pages, and ask if the analyst would trust it on the next bid. A wrong omission is expensive and each source document needs provenance. [GovTribe prices and coverage](https://govtribe.com/plans) · [BamSEC pricing](https://www.bamsec.com/pricing) · [SAM opportunity API](https://open.gsa.gov/api/get-opportunities-public-api/)

**Highest speculative upside:** agent-purchased specialized services could turn a good data product into something many other products buy automatically. The bottleneck is finding an answer valuable enough to buy repeatedly. Trophic Arena includes an agent entry point because competing with bots is part of the game. We would add billing only after an organizer or bot builder requests ongoing access. [Cloudflare gateway launch](https://blog.cloudflare.com/monetization-gateway-beta/) · [Chainalysis transaction analysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/x402-agentic-payments-adoption/)

## Why build this one?

A reviewer can enter a forecast, get a score and rerun the exact world under a different ecological rule in one sitting. A second developer can bring a bot and test whether a new strategy beats the baseline on fresh seeds. Both are observable technical outcomes without access to a customer’s private data. The main commercial risk is whether the same people come back after the novelty of the first round.

Academic projects establish the neighborhood. Sakana AI and its collaborators have searched artificial-life simulations for interesting emergent behaviors. ForecastBench evaluates probabilities on real-world questions that resolve over time. This project offers a smaller, controlled problem where the future resolves on demand and the full history can be replayed. Its scores measure forecast skill inside this particular simulator. [ASAL research](https://pub.sakana.ai/asal/) · [ForecastBench methodology](https://forecastbench.org/about/)

## What would make the arena a business?

Sell a $249 facilitated private session for up to 30 players. The host runs several worlds, participants can bring a bot, and each room settles in front of the group. Source this first through a club officer or a hackathon organizer who already has a scheduled event. A competing event platform's price makes the ask plausible. A prepayment or deposit would establish much more. [Single-event price comparison](https://support.goosechase.com/en/articles/4571290-how-much-does-a-goosechase-experience-cost)

| Illustrative per-event economics | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Price charged | $249 |
| Hosting, processing and incidental costs assumed | $12 |
| Two hours of sales plus two hours of prep/delivery at $40/hour | $160 |
| Contribution after those costs | **$77** |

At these assumptions, 16 events recover an illustrative $1,200 in initial development time. At six total founder hours per sale and event, contribution turns negative ($249 − $12 − $240 = −$3). This scenario excludes travel, taxes and ongoing fixed overhead. It is sensitive to sales labor and does not establish net profit or a date for reaching it.

**Two-week buying test.** Offer a short playable demo to ten organizers with an event already on their calendar. Ask for a $249 booking. Track time from first contact through setup and facilitation, plus repeat bookings. Continue investing if two unrelated organizers pay, one asks for a second round on a later date, and actual contribution is positive at the founder's chosen hourly rate. If players love it but organizers decline, the entertainment signal and buying signal have diverged. No customer interviews, bookings, outreach or payments were part of this research.

**Expansion if that works.** Private leagues and sponsored bot tournaments give organizers a reason to return. An agent-evaluation product would require evidence that distinct strategies separate reliably on unseen worlds across multiple environments. A win on the bundled simulator measures performance on that simulator. Established benchmarks already cover prediction on real-world events. [ForecastBench](https://forecastbench.org/about/) · [Metaculus bot series](https://www.metaculus.com/aib/2026/spring/)
